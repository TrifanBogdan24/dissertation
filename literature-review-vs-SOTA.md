The core difference lies in their purpose and scope: a **literature review** maps the intellectual foundation and historical progression of your topic, while the **state of the art (SOTA)** establishes the current frontier of performance, methodologies, or technology.

| Dimension | Literature Review | State of the Art (SOTA) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary Goal** | Contextualize the research, trace theoretical debates, and identify knowledge gaps | Benchmark existing top-tier solutions to prove your contribution advances beyond them |
| **Time Horizon** | Broad (historical foundations up to the present) | Narrow (the most recent 2–5 years of current breakthroughs) |
| **Focus** | Theories, concepts, paradigms, and academic consensus/disagreements | Implementations, empirical benchmarks, algorithms, and practical performance |
| **Typical Disciplines** | Universal across Humanities, Social Sciences, STEM, and Medicine | Most prevalent in Computer Science, Engineering, Data Science, and Applied Sciences |
| **Role in the Thesis** | Answers: *"What has been thought and researched about this problem historically?"* | Answers: *"What is the absolute best existing method today, and how does my work beat or improve upon it?"* |

---

### Literature Review: Mapping the Landscape

A literature review builds the academic context for your investigation. It explains how your research question came to exist by synthesizing prior work.

* **Traces evolution:** Demonstrates how theories, frameworks, and methodologies have evolved over decades.
* **Critiques perspectives:** Weighs competing schools of thought against one another rather than just reporting specs.
* **Justifies relevance:** Highlights unresolved questions, blind spots, or methodological limitations in prior research that motivate your study.

### State of the Art: Defining the Frontier

The state of the art acts as a concrete benchmark. It defines the current high-water mark of technology or practice that your work must be evaluated against.

* **Focuses on concrete metrics:** Compares state-of-the-art models or tools using standardized datasets, execution speed, error rates, precision/recall, or material durability.
* **Establishes a baseline:** Outlines the specific existing models, architectures, or protocols you implement as baselines in your experimental section.
* **Frames novelty:** Clarifies the exact delta between the cutting edge and your proposed contribution.

---

### How They Fit Together in a Dissertation

* **Integrated Structure:** In many academic fields (especially outside applied engineering and machine learning), the state of the art is simply the **final subsection of the literature review chapter**, narrowing down from broad theoretical foundations to today’s cutting-edge approaches.
* **Separated Structure:** In applied fields (such as deep learning or systems engineering), the **literature review** forms an early chapter outlining problem formulations and foundational theory, while the **state of the art / baseline review** sits closer to the methodology or experimental setup to justify model architecture choices and comparison baselines.
