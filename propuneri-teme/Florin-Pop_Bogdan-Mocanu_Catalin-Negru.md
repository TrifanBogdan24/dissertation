## 1. Scheduling, Resource Allocation and Optimization in Continuum Computing

Scheduling and optimization in continuum computing focus on intelligently allocating and coordinating
computational, memory, storage, networking, and energy resources across highly heterogeneous Cloud,
Edge, and IoT infrastructures. The dynamic nature of continuum environments makes conventional centralized
scheduling approaches insufficient, particularly when applications have strict latency, energy,
reliability, security, and resource constraints. Research in this direction investigates distributed,
adaptive, data-driven scheduling algorithms and models that can make decisions in environments where
resource availability, network conditions, workload characteristics, and application requirements change
continuously. Multi-objective optimization represents a central research challenge, requiring the
simultaneous consideration of performance, energy consumption, cost, reliability, security, and quality
of service.

- **T1.** Research and develop intelligent scheduling algorithms using machine learning and optimization
  techniques to dynamically allocate workloads across Cloud and Edge resources.
- **T2.** Research and develop scheduling models that simultaneously optimize latency, energy
  consumption, resource utilization, cost, reliability, and security.
- **T3.** Research and develop deadline-aware scheduling algorithms capable of guaranteeing timing
  requirements for real-time and safety-critical applications.
- **T4.** Research and develop scheduling mechanisms capable of adapting to changing workloads, resource
  availability, network conditions, failures, and node mobility.
- **T5.** Research and develop scalable scheduling algorithms that operate without relying on a single
  centralized controller and remain effective in large continuum infrastructures.

## 2. Cybersecurity for Modern Distributed Systems

Cybersecurity in Distributed Systems addresses the protection of highly heterogeneous, distributed,
dynamic, and interconnected computing environments such as data centers, Edge nodes, IoT devices, mobile
platforms, and cyber-physical infrastructures. Unlike traditional centralized infrastructures, Continuum
Computing introduces new security challenges related to heterogeneity, mobility, dynamic service placement,
data processing, and the large number of potentially untrusted components. Research in this area focuses
on developing adaptive, context-aware security mechanisms that continuously assess the security of
computational resources, services, communications, and data.

- **T1.** Research and develop adaptive security mechanisms that dynamically adjust protection levels
  according to the security context, resource capabilities, workload characteristics, and threat
  landscape of continuum environments.
- **T2.** Research and develop decentralized trust and reputation models for continuously evaluating the
  reliability and security of nodes, services, devices, and data sources.
- **T3.** Research and design zero-trust security architectures based on continuous authentication,
  authorization, device attestation, and dynamic access control across distributed continuum resources.
- **T4.** Research, design, and integrate security risk, trust, and threat information into resource
  allocation, service placement, migration, and orchestration decisions.
- **T5.** Research and develop privacy-preserving mechanisms for distributed data processing and AI,
  combining techniques such as federated learning, secure aggregation, differential privacy, and
  confidential computing.
- **T6.** Research and develop autonomous mechanisms for attack detection, compromised-resource
  isolation, service recovery, and dynamic system reconfiguration following security incidents.

## 3. Robustness of AI Systems

Adversarial robustness of AI systems examines the reliability and security of AI models when exposed to
intentionally manipulated inputs, poisoned training data, model manipulation, distribution shifts, or
other adversarial conditions. The growing deployment of AI models at the edge and in autonomous
cyber-physical systems creates significant security risks because AI-generated decisions may directly
influence physical actions, resource allocation, threat detection, or mission-critical operations.
Research in this area addresses the complete AI lifecycle, including secure data acquisition, model
training, deployment, runtime monitoring, and adaptation. An important direction is developing mechanisms
to detect adversarial inputs and model anomalies at runtime while maintaining the computational efficiency
required by edge platforms.

- **T1.** Research and develop mechanisms for detecting and mitigating poisoning, backdoor,
  malicious-client, and model-manipulation attacks in federated learning environments.
- **T2.** Research and develop methods for assessing, explaining, and formally or empirically verifying the
  robustness and reliability of AI decisions under adversarial conditions.
- **T3.** Research and develop lightweight runtime mechanisms for detecting adversarial inputs, distribution
  shifts, anomalous model behavior, and degradation of AI performance.
- **T4.** Research and develop AI-driven SOAR (Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response) mechanisms
  that integrate heterogeneous security tools and automate the complete incident-response lifecycle,
  reducing response time and human error while improving cyber-resilience.
- **T5.** Research and develop intelligent deception environments that dynamically adapt their behavior
  to attacker actions and generate useful intelligence for threat detection and analysis.
- **T6.** Research and develop AI-driven cyber-range environments in which offensive and defensive agents
  interact to evaluate, optimize, and continuously improve cybersecurity strategies.

## 4. Intelligent Monitoring, Modeling, and Sustainable Management of Natural Resources

This theme aims to research and develop advanced methods, architectures, and digital technologies for the
observation, integration, analysis, modeling, prediction, and sustainable management of natural resources,
using heterogeneous data sources and technologies such as Earth Observation, IoT, Edge-Cloud computing,
Artificial Intelligence, Digital Twins, and interoperable data infrastructures.

The main objective is to create a coherent research chain:
> **Observe** $\rightarrow$ **Integrate** $\rightarrow$ **Process** $\rightarrow$ **Understand** $\rightarrow$ **Model** $\rightarrow$ **Predict** $\rightarrow$ **Decide** $\rightarrow$ **Manage sustainably**

and to investigate how heterogeneous data from satellites, IoT/edge sensors, drones, meteorological stations,
and existing environmental databases can be integrated to continuously monitor natural resources and
support management decisions.

- **T1. Earth Observation and Remote Sensing for Natural Resource Monitoring** - Satellite, aerial, UAV,
  and multispectral/hyperspectral data for monitoring land, forests, water, soil, vegetation, and
  ecosystem changes.
- **T2. IoT and In-Situ Environmental Monitoring** - Sensor networks and IoT systems for continuous
  measurement of environmental and natural-resource parameters.
- **T3. Multi-Source Environmental Data Integration and Interoperability** - Integration of satellite, IoT,
  geospatial, meteorological, hydrological, and socioeconomic data using interoperable standards and FAIR
  principles.
- **T4. AI and Machine Learning for Environmental Intelligence** - Classification, anomaly detection,
  change detection, forecasting, pattern discovery, and predictive modeling of environmental processes.
- **T5. Cloud-Edge Computing for Environmental Monitoring** - Distributed architectures for scalable,
  near-real-time processing of large environmental and Earth Observation datasets.
- **T6. Digital Twins of Natural Resources and Ecosystems** - Dynamic digital representations of forests,
  watersheds, agricultural areas, wetlands, protected areas, and other ecosystems for simulation and
  forecasting.
- **T7. Biodiversity and Ecosystem Monitoring** - Data-driven methods for habitat mapping, biodiversity
  assessment, ecosystem health indicators, and detection of ecological changes.
- **T8. Intelligent Decision-Support Systems for Sustainable Resource Management** - Combining observations,
  models, AI, and simulations to support authorities, researchers, and resource managers.
- **T9. Agentic AI for Environmental Monitoring and Management** - Autonomous and collaborative AI agents
  for data acquisition, analysis, environmental assessment, forecasting, alerting, and decision support.
- **T10. Open, FAIR, and Trusted Environmental Data Spaces** - Data governance, provenance, quality,
  metadata, security, access control, reproducibility, and sharing of environmental data.
